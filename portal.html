    Send everything at once

    For when labelling a hundred requests is the thing standing between you and starting.

    Read this first — it is a real trade-off, not a formality.

    A document that arrives against the request it answers tells us what it is. A bulk drop means we work that out ourselves, from the file: what it is, which entity and period it covers, and which of the requests below it was meant to satisfy.

    That is slower. It also makes us less likely to notice what is missing — the gap analysis is only as good as knowing what was meant to be there, and a folder does not tell us what is not in it. A request you skip with a reason leaves a mark in the report. A request nothing happens to, because nothing in the drop obviously answered it, can be read as answered when it was not.

    It matters most on the 33 core requests. Those are the ones the review cannot proceed without, and the ones where guessing wrong costs the most.

    Use it anyway if it is the difference between starting and not starting. A bulk drop today beats a tidy intake that never happens, and you can come back and answer the core requests specifically afterwards — the two work perfectly well together.
