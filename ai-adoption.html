Then: where it earns, and what you allow

The two jobs that sit either side of the build. Priced and delivered separately, and neither commits you to the other.

PUTTING IT TO WORK

For when you want the productivity and have nobody who can tell you where it is real.

  • Where it would actually earn. Which work in your operation is genuinely worth automating, judged against how the business runs rather than against what is currently fashionable.
  • Buy, build or neither. What a tool costs to run at your volume, what it would cost to build, and which of the obvious applications will not repay the effort. Often the honest answer is the third one.
  • Delivered so it survives you. Documented, owned by your people, and not dependent on us or on a vendor you cannot leave.

THE POLICY — $18,000

For when somebody is going to ask what you allow, and you need an answer you can stand behind.

  • What is already in use. An inventory from what the network and the expense claims show, not a survey asking people to confess.
  • A policy people will follow. Written to permit rather than forbid: named tools, what may go into each, what must never, who signs an exception. A ban is ignored by the second week and leaves you the same exposure plus a document saying you knew.
  • The evidence behind it. Processing agreements, retention terms, approvals and an audit trail.

THE GOVERNANCE — $28,000

For when a document is no longer enough, because the question has stopped being what you allow and started being who decided.

  • Every model and AI-bearing vendor, inventoried. Classified by what it touches and what it could cost you. Most of the exposure is in software you already bought, where a vendor turned a feature on.
  • Decision rights, and an approval path people use. The long one is why shadow AI exists.
  • Monitoring, an incident procedure, and a board pack. Mapped to the NIST AI Risk Management Framework and ISO/IEC 42001, and to the EU AI Act where you have European exposure — as a structure the work is delivered under, never as a certification.
