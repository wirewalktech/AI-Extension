Human services and behavioural health

The money arrived with conditions. The conditions outlive the money.

Funded work is not paid work. A cost ruled unallowable two years after it was spent is repayable now, out of this year's budget, by an organisation that had no margin in the first place. A review reads programme economics and the compliance record together, because for you they are the same document.

Where it concentrates here

6 losses that recur in a human services organisation and are worth going looking for. Each is concentrated — a single agreement, a single queue, a single account nobody reviews — which is precisely why a sample of the operation tends to miss them and a full pass tends to find them. They are not findings from a named engagement; they are what a review of a human services organisation is built to look for.

  1. Service units delivered, units billed and units authorised described by three different numbers, with nobody owning the distance between them.
  2. A cost allocation plan approved years ago and applied by habit since, so indirect recovery drifts from what the plan actually permits.
  3. Medicaid recoupments after payment — for eligibility that lapsed, supervision that was not documented, or a service note that did not meet the standard — absorbed as bad debt rather than traced to the process that caused them.
  4. Agency and overtime cover for vacancies, priced into no programme budget, quietly turning a funded programme into a subsidised one.
  5. Programmes cross-subsidising each other invisibly, because the true cost of each is never assembled and the board sees only the consolidated position.
  6. Single audit findings closed on paper with a corrective action nobody operationalised, which is how the same finding recurs three years running.

Not every human services organisation is the same business

  • Behavioural health and substance useMedical necessity documentation and supervision notes that make a delivered service billable or retroactively unbillable.
  • Developmental disability servicesResidential and day programmes on fixed rates set years ago, against staffing costs that moved with the labour market.
  • Child and family servicesMultiple funders per client, each with its own eligibility, unit definition and reporting calendar.
  • Housing and homelessness servicesBraided funding where one cost may be allowable to one funder and not another, and the allocation decision is rarely documented.
  • Workforce and community programmesOutcome-based contracts where payment depends on a result recorded months after the service was delivered.

Where this data actually lives

  • Netsmart, Credible or Qualifacts as the EHR; Apricot or ETO for programme outcomes
  • A separate billing intermediary or clearinghouse between you and the state
  • Payroll and scheduling that do not talk to the service-unit record, so authorised, delivered and paid are three different systems
  • A cost allocation workbook in Excel that is the real basis of every indirect claim

Named because it changes what the intake asks for. An extract from one of these has known gaps, and the review is built around them rather than surprised by them.

The obligations that set the deadlines

  • Single audit under Uniform Guidance, and whether prior findings actually closed
  • Medicaid cost reports and rate-setting submissions
  • State licensing and accreditation surveys — CARF, COA or Joint Commission
  • Staff credentialling, supervision and background check records per programme
  • Form 990 and functional expense allocation, which the same allocation plan drives

What the review would ask you for

8 requests written for a human services organisation and asked of nobody else — 5 of them core. They sit alongside the operating spine every business has — finance, revenue, receivables, payables, contracts, people, IT, operations, risk, commercial and governance — which is 107 further requests.
  • Funding sources: contracts, grants and rate agreements Core Per funder, with period, ceiling and the reporting each requires.
  • Cost allocation plan and indirect rate agreement Core The approved plan, and how it is actually applied month to month.
  • Service units delivered against units billed and units authorised Core Three numbers that describe the same service; the gaps are the finding.
  • Medicaid denials and recoupments, by reason Core Including anything recouped after payment.
  • Staff credentialling and supervision records Core A service delivered by an unqualified or unsupervised staff member is unbillable retroactively.
  • Single audit (Uniform Guidance) reports and findings Last two years, with the corrective action and whether it closed.
  • Client eligibility documentation and redetermination tracking Lapsed eligibility is the most common cause of retroactive denial.
  • Vacancy, turnover and agency-staffing cost by programme Agency cover is often the difference between a programme that funds itself and one that does not.

When this stops being interesting and becomes urgent

  • A recoupment demand after a state audit
  • A rate rebase or a contract renewal at a rate that no longer covers staffing
  • A single audit finding, especially a repeat one
  • A merger conversation with another provider
  • Losing a funder and discovering which shared costs it was carrying
