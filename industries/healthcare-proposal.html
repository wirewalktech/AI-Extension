Proposal — Healthcare

Denied once, and then never touched again.

This is what we would actually do, in what order, and what it costs. No discovery call is required to see the number.

What we would go looking for

These are patterns that recur in healthcare, written as things to look for. They are not findings from a named engagement.

  1. Denials by reason code and payer showing one or two causes carrying most of the value, with no owner assigned to either.
  2. Denied claims never reworked at all, because the queue exceeded the team's capacity and the oldest simply aged out.
  3. Contracted rate against paid rate never systematically compared, so underpayment is invisible unless a payer volunteers it.
  4. Credentialing and enrolment adding weeks between a clinician's start date and their first billable encounter.
  5. Preference-item supply variation between clinicians doing the same procedure, with no clinical difference in outcome.

The sequence

Five weeks for one operating unit. More units run in parallel rather than in series, so a group does not take five times as long.

  • Week 1
    Intake and reconciliationYou complete the intake in the client portal — 9 requests written for healthcare and asked of nobody else, alongside the 107 that cover the operating spine any business has. Anything you do not have is skipped in one click, with a reason. We reconcile what arrives against what was asked and tell you, before any fieldwork, which findings the gaps will limit.
  • Weeks 2–3
    Full pass, not a sampleEvery encounter in scope is read rather than sampled. Concentrated loss is exactly what sampling misses — one contract, one queue, one unreviewed account — so a sample of the operation tends to miss it and a full pass tends to find it.
  • Week 4
    Quantification and challengeEach finding is sized, and each is put to the person who owns it before it is written down. A finding the operator can immediately explain away is not a finding, and it is cheaper to discover that here than in the room.
  • Week 5
    Report and walkthroughA written report with the evidence attached, and a walkthrough with whoever you want in the room. Findings are ranked by annual value and by how hard they are to act on, because those are different axes and the cheap ones should not wait for the big ones.

What you get

  • A written report, with the evidence for each finding attached rather than referenced.
  • Each finding sized in annual value, and ranked against how hard it is to act on.
  • The reconciliation of what was asked for against what was provided, so the limits of the work are stated rather than implied.
  • A walkthrough with whoever you want present.
  • Your documents destroyed on completion, with a certificate naming every file.

Phase one is priced before phase two is discussed. The report is yours either way, and we take no vendor commissions or referral fees on anything we recommend.

What we need from you

4 core requests written for healthcare, inside the full intake. Anything you do not have can be skipped with a reason — skipping narrows what the review can conclude, and we tell you where before the work starts rather than after.

  • Claims detail — Line level for the period. De-identified wherever possible — read the personal data note before uploading anything patient-identifiable.
  • Denials by reason code, payer and month
  • Payer contracts and fee schedules — Including escalators, effective dates and termination notice.
  • Provider credentialing and enrolment — Time from application to first billable encounter, and where it waits.

If any of this is already happening

The review is always worth doing and rarely urgent. It becomes urgent when something else is happening — and if one of these is, the sequence above compresses to fit it.

  • A private equity approach or a DSO/MSO roll-up
  • A payer contract coming up for renegotiation without a rate analysis to argue from
  • A denial rate that moved and nobody can say why
  • An acquisition where the acquired site's economics turn out to be different
  • A HIPAA risk analysis requested by a payer, a lender or a buyer

Who needs to be in the room

  • Signs it: Practice administrator or CEO; the managing physician on clinical scope
  • Will resist it: Physician owners whose compensation formula the review will decompose — worth telling them what the review is for before they hear it from somewhere else.
  • Has been asking for it: The revenue cycle lead, who can already name the problem sites

What it costs

Priced by operating unit — an entity, a location, or a business the accounts are kept separately for. The second unit costs less than the first because by then the intake is built, the chart of accounts is understood and the patterns are known.
