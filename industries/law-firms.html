Law firms

The gap between recorded, billed and collected.

Three numbers describe the same hour of work, and in most firms nobody owns the distance between them. A review that reads matter economics end to end finds where the hour was lost, and whether it was lost in the recording, the billing or the collecting.

The terms above are the ones the review uses with you, because they are the ones you already use. Nothing is translated into a consultant's vocabulary and then translated back.

Where it concentrates here

Five losses that recur in law firms and are worth going looking for. Each is concentrated — a single agreement, a single queue, a single account nobody reviews — which is precisely why a sample of the operation tends to miss them and a full pass tends to find them. They are not findings from a named engagement; they are what a review of law firms is built to look for.

  1. Time recorded and left in WIP past the point any client will pay it, ageing quietly because writing it off requires a conversation nobody wants.
  2. A realisation rate that is reported firm-wide and never decomposed, so the two practice groups dragging it down are invisible in the average.
  3. Disbursements incurred on a client's behalf and never recharged — small individually, and continuous.
  4. Panel rate agreements with volume commitments or agreed discounts that nobody tracks against actual billings.
  5. Trust and client account reconciliation exceptions carried month to month, which is a regulatory exposure rather than an accounting untidiness.

Where this data actually lives

  • Elite 3E or ProLaw for the larger firms; Aderant Expert where the finance function grew up around it
  • Clio Manage or PracticePanther in firms under about forty fee earners
  • Chrome River or Nexudus for disbursements, usually reconciled by hand
  • iManage or NetDocuments for matter files — often the only complete record of what was actually agreed
  • A separate IOLTA/client account reconciliation, frequently in Excel

Named because it changes what the intake asks for. An extract from one of these has known gaps, and the review is built around them rather than surprised by them.

The obligations that set the deadlines

  • State bar trust account (IOLTA) compliance and the annual certification
  • Client account three-way reconciliation — bank, ledger and matter balances
  • Malpractice insurance renewal disclosures on claims and circumstances
  • 1099 reporting on settlement disbursements

What the review would ask you for

9 requests written for law firms and asked of nobody else — 4 of them core. They sit alongside the operating spine every business has — finance, revenue, receivables, payables, contracts, people, IT, operations, risk, commercial and governance — which is 107 further requests.
  • Matter list with fee arrangement Core Hourly, fixed, contingent or capped, by practice group.
  • Billed, collected and written down by matter Core Recorded time, billed time and collected fee are three different numbers.
  • Work in progress and unbilled time Core Aged by matter and fee earner.
  • Client and trust account reconciliation Core Balances, ageing and unclaimed funds. Reconciliation exceptions here are a regulatory matter, not merely an accounting one.
  • Leverage and utilisation by grade Partner, associate and paralegal hours against target.
  • Origination and credit allocation How work is credited, and what that drives.
  • Conflicts checking process and turnaround
  • Disbursements and expense recovery What is incurred and never recharged.
  • Panel appointments and rate agreements Including any agreed discounts and volumes.

Anything you do not have can be skipped in one click, with a reason. The intake is completed in the client portal, which saves each answer as it is given and can be returned to as often as you like.

When this stops being interesting and becomes urgent

  • A partner retiring or de-equitising, which forces a real valuation of WIP and receivables rather than a comfortable one
  • A merger conversation, where the other side's diligence asks the questions nobody internal has asked
  • A bar audit or a trust account exception that did not clear
  • A practice management migration, which surfaces every unbilled matter at once
  • A bank covenant test on the firm's line of credit

Who this involves
