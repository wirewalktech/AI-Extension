Where it concentrates here

Five losses that recur in insurance and are worth going looking for. Each is concentrated — a single agreement, a single queue, a single account nobody reviews — which is precisely why a sample of the operation tends to miss them and a full pass tends to find them. They are not findings from a named engagement; they are what a review of insurance is built to look for.

  1. Claims leakage — settlements above what the policy required — concentrated in particular claim types or handlers.
  2. Acquisition cost and commission overrides negotiated historically and never revisited against the business each channel now produces.
  3. Underwriting referrals in volumes that suggest authority limits are set wrongly rather than that risks are unusual.
  4. Reconciliation and bordereaux processing absorbing effort disproportionate to the premium involved.
  5. Reserve development patterns that repeat by line and are treated as actuarial rather than operational.

Not every insurance is the same business

  • Commercial lines brokerageCommission on premium that moves with the market, and service cost that does not — a hardening market flatters results that a softening one will not.
  • Employee benefitsPer-employee-per-month fees against client headcount that changes constantly and is reconciled annually at best.
  • Personal linesHigh account count, low premium, where cost to serve per policy is almost never calculated and is frequently above the commission.
  • Programme and MGA businessDelegated authority, binding rights and carrier audit exposure on a book someone else underwrites.
  • Contingent and profit-sharing incomeMaterial, volatile, recognised inconsistently, and the first thing an acquirer normalises away.

Where this data actually lives

  • Applied Epic or Vertafore AMS360 as the agency management system
  • A separate commission download reconciliation, frequently in Excel
  • EZLynx or Applied Rater for personal lines comparative quoting
  • Carrier portals holding the authoritative policy record, which the AMS mirrors imperfectly

Named because it changes what the intake asks for. An extract from one of these has known gaps, and the review is built around them rather than surprised by them.

The obligations that set the deadlines

  • Producer licensing and appointments per state per carrier, and their renewals
  • Surplus lines tax filings and diligent search affidavits
  • Carrier audits of delegated binding authority
  • E&O renewal disclosures, which ask about claims and procedures
  • Fiduciary handling of premium trust accounts where the state requires it

What the review would ask you for

7 further requests, specific to insurance — 3 of them core — on top of the 107 the intake asks of every organisation, across finance, revenue, receivables, payables, contracts, people, IT, operations, risk, commercial and governance.

When this stops being interesting and becomes urgent

  • An acquisition approach — brokerage consolidation is constant, and the buyer's first question is organic growth stripped of contingents
  • A carrier appointment lost, or a book moved
  • A producer leaving with accounts, testing what the agreements actually say
  • A commission reconciliation gap nobody can close
  • Perpetuation, where an internal buyer needs a defensible valuation

Who this involves

  • Signs it: Principal or agency president
  • Resists it: Producers whose book economics and service cost become visible
  • Has been asking for it: The operations lead, who knows which accounts consume the service team
