Manufacturing

Standard cost said one thing. The line said another.

Product margin is calculated from standards, and standards are refreshed less often than anyone admits. When they drift, every downstream decision — what to quote, what to promote, what to discontinue — is made on numbers that stopped being true.

  • standard cost
  • yield
  • scrap and rework
  • OEE
  • BOM
  • obsolescence
  • accessorials
  • takt

The terms above are the ones the review uses with you, because they are the ones you already use. Nothing is translated into a consultant's vocabulary and then translated back.

Where it concentrates here

Five losses that recur in manufacturing and are worth going looking for. Each is concentrated — a single agreement, a single queue, a single account nobody reviews — which is precisely why a sample of the operation tends to miss them and a full pass tends to find them. They are not findings from a named engagement; they are what a review of manufacturing is built to look for.

  1. Standard costs last genuinely rebuilt several years ago, so reported margin by product is a well-formatted guess.
  2. Scrap and rework booked to a variance account that is reviewed in total and never by line, so a single bad process hides inside an acceptable aggregate.
  3. Freight accessorials — detention, demurrage, fuel, redelivery — turning a negotiated rate into a materially different paid rate.
  4. Obsolete stock carried at cost because writing it down needs an authorisation nobody wants to sign.
  5. A single-source component with no alternate qualified, priced accordingly and reviewed never.

7 further requests, specific to manufacturing — 3 of them core — on top of the 107 the intake asks of every organisation, across finance, revenue, receivables, payables, contracts, people, IT, operations, risk, commercial and governance.
