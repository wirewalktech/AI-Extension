Where it concentrates here

Five losses that recur in healthcare and are worth going looking for. Each is concentrated — a single agreement, a single queue, a single account nobody reviews — which is precisely why a sample of the operation tends to miss them and a full pass tends to find them. They are not findings from a named engagement; they are what a review of healthcare is built to look for.

  1. Denials by reason code and payer showing one or two causes carrying most of the value, with no owner assigned to either.
  2. Denied claims never reworked at all, because the queue exceeded the team's capacity and the oldest simply aged out.
  3. Contracted rate against paid rate never systematically compared, so underpayment is invisible unless a payer volunteers it.
  4. Credentialing and enrolment adding weeks between a clinician's start date and their first billable encounter.
  5. Preference-item supply variation between clinicians doing the same procedure, with no clinical difference in outcome.

Where this data actually lives

  • Epic in the larger groups; athenahealth, eClinicalWorks or NextGen in the mid-market; Modernizing Medicine in specialty practices
  • Waystar, Availity or Change Healthcare sitting between the practice and the payer
  • A separate credentialling tracker, very often a spreadsheet
  • Dentrix or Open Dental in dental groups, per location and rarely consolidated

Named because it changes what the intake asks for. An extract from one of these has known gaps, and the review is built around them rather than surprised by them.

Reported in your numbers, not ours

  • chair or room utilisation

The obligations that set the deadlines

  • Payer credentialling and revalidation per provider per payer — lapse and the claims deny
  • Medicare cost reports and Medicaid cost settlement where applicable
  • HIPAA security risk analysis, which is required annually and often is not done
  • Stark and Anti-Kickback exposure on any physician compensation formula
  • 340B compliance where the group participates

When this stops being interesting and becomes urgent

  • A private equity approach or a DSO/MSO roll-up
  • A payer contract coming up for renegotiation without a rate analysis to argue from
  • A denial rate that moved and nobody can say why
  • An acquisition where the acquired site's economics turn out to be different
  • A HIPAA risk analysis requested by a payer, a lender or a buyer

Who this involves

  • Signs it: Practice administrator or CEO; the managing physician on clinical scope
  • Resists it: Physician owners whose compensation formula the review will decompose
  • Has been asking for it: The revenue cycle lead, who can already name the problem sites
